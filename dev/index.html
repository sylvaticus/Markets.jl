Markets.jl

Pre-release: under active development and validation

This project is at a proof-of-concept stage and has not been released. A substantial part of it was generated with the assistance of an AI model and is still being reviewed. Both the modelling framework (its assumptions and equations) and its software implementation are currently undergoing verification and validation.

  • Results have not yet been validated and should not be used for publications, policy advice, or operational decisions.
  • The API may change without notice and without backward compatibility.

Markets.jl solves a spatial partial-equilibrium model of an economic sector made of an arbitrary set of products, regions and transformation processes. You describe the economy with data — demand and supply curves, processes that turn some products into others, transport costs between regions — and a single optimisation returns production, consumption, trade and prices for every region and product.

The engine knows no specific product or sector. The same data types describe any sector with primary supply, transformation and trade: an oil refinery splitting crude into petrol, diesel and fuel oil, a steel chain from iron ore to cars, a fishery under quota, a dairy turning milk into cheese. Two worked examples ship with the package: the dairy, as the smallest model that is still a market, and the forest-products sector (roundwood → sawnwood, panels, pulp, paper and pellets, with sawmill residues recycled and smallwood either burnt or milled) at full size.

GitHub: https://github.com/sylvaticus/Markets.jl

Features

  • Market equilibrium as an optimisation. The competitive equilibrium is computed as the maximum of net social surplus. Quantities are the primal solution; prices are the duals of the material-balance constraints.
  • Multi-stage transformation with joint products. A process can have several outputs (e.g. a main product and a residue), and residues can be inputs to other processes.
  • Smooth input substitution. Inputs grouped in a CES nest substitute for each other continuously as relative prices change; fixed-proportion (Leontief) inputs are also available.
  • Linked regional markets. Regions trade at a transport cost, so equilibrium prices differ across regions by at most that cost.
  • Imperfect substitution between origins, where you want it. Give a product an Armington elasticity and its regional varieties become imperfect substitutes: regions cross-haul and every region's price responds to supply and demand everywhere. An infinite elasticity, the default, is the homogeneous case above.
  • Limits, in one API. Plant capacities, port and pipeline ceilings, quotas, licences, resources shared between products, minimum shares: any linear constraint on the model's quantities is a Limit, written with @limit as the inequality it stands for, and a binding one reports its rent — on the limit, and on every quantity it charges.
  • Substitution in final demand. Products that compete for the same use are grouped in a nest, so one product's price moves another's demand — and substitutes outside the model enter as curve shifters.
  • Taxes and subsidies. Wedges on production, consumption, transformation or trade, per unit or ad valorem, with the revenue of each reported.
  • Tidy results. Every result is a DataFrame.

Installation

using Pkg
Pkg.add(url = "https://github.com/sylvaticus/Markets.jl")   # this model

Pkg.add("Markets") would install the registered v0.0.1 instead, which is the older and entirely different formulation described in Previous formulation (v0.0.1); its documentation is kept under the v0.0.2 entry of the version selector.

Quick start

using Markets
include(joinpath(pkgdir(Markets), "examples", "minimal", "minimal_market.jl"))
result.prices

That script is the Two regions and a dairy page, generated from the source with Literate.jl, as is the larger forest example.
