# Modelling choices

These pages explain what the model assumes and why. The exact program that the
code builds is on the [Code implementation](@ref) page.

* [Equilibrium, curves and welfare](@ref): why one optimisation gives the
  market equilibrium, the shape of the demand and supply curves, how everything
  other than the price moves them, and what the welfare the model reports can
  and cannot be used for.
* [Processes and substitution](@ref): transformations and their input nests,
  joint products, and substitution between products in final demand.
* [Taxes and trade](@ref): wedges between prices, transport between regions,
  and imperfect substitution between origins.
* [Accounts, calibration and scope](@ref): what the model leaves
  unconstrained, the physical accounts it keeps, calibration to a base year,
  and what lies outside the engine.

## Where these ideas come from

The works behind the choices on these pages, and the code they can be read in,
are collected on the [References](@ref) page: a theory list cited as
*Author (year)*, and an implementation list cited as *[C1]*, *[C2]* and so on,
pointing at repositories where the same concept is written out and runs.
